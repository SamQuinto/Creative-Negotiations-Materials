# Survey measure and design notes

This note records where the survey measures came from and which questions were written specifically for this project. The participant-facing YAML files do not display citations.

## Class reflection survey

| Questions | What they do | Source status |
|---|---|---|
| Q1–Q2 | Check whether students tried to understand and imagine the negotiation from the other party's perspective. | Situational adaptations informed by the cognitive perspective-taking tradition, especially Davis (1983) and Galinsky et al. (2008). These are brief study-specific checks, not the full Interpersonal Reactivity Index. |
| Q3 | Asks whether students translated their understanding of the other party into questions, offers or responses. | Project-developed behavioral-enactment item. It deliberately distinguishes understanding a perspective from acting on it. |
| Q4 | Checks attention to one's own role and information. | Project-developed comparison item for the Control versus Perspective Taking classroom exercise. |
| Q5 | Measures satisfaction with one's own negotiation outcome. | Subjective Value Inventory item from Curhan, Elfenbein and Xu (2006). |
| Q6–Q8 | Compare satisfaction, realism and ability to understand the other party with students' earlier fixed-issue negotiations. | Project-developed comparative items. They should be analyzed individually rather than described as a validated scale. |
| Q9–Q13 | Ask students to compare strategies, connect the exercise to course principles, identify a transferable action and choose useful debrief activities. | Instructor-developed reflection prompts. Their sequence follows the experiential-learning logic of moving from experience to reflection, conceptual interpretation and future application. They are teaching prompts, not a psychometric scale. |

## Prolific research survey

| Questions | What they do | Source status |
|---|---|---|
| Q1–Q16 | Measure feelings about the outcome, self, process and relationship. | Full 16-item Subjective Value Inventory from Curhan, Elfenbein and Xu (2006). Q3 and Q5 are reverse-scored. |
| Q17–Q19 | Check whether the task was imaginable, realistic and taken seriously. | Project-developed experience and data-quality checks; analyze separately. |
| Q20–Q24 | Measure idea-generation engagement during the negotiation. | Five-item idea-generation component adapted to the negotiation from Zhang and Bartol's (2010) Creative Process Engagement measure. |
| Q25–Q27 | Measure confidence in solving problems creatively, developing another person's ideas and generating novel ideas. | Three-item Creative Self-Efficacy measure from Tierney and Farmer (2002), adapted to refer to this negotiation. |
| Q28–Q32 | Record whether a temporary impasse occurred, what it concerned, when it occurred, whether it was overcome and how. | Adapted from the post-negotiation protocol in Loewenstein and Brett (2007). The open responses can be coded for strategic flexibility, persistence, discussion of interests and introduction of additional issues. |
| Q33 | Collects optional feedback. | Project-developed. |
