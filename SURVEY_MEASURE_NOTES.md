# Survey measure and design notes

This note records where the survey measures came from and which questions were written specifically for this project. The participant-facing YAML files do not display citations.

## Class reflection survey

| Questions | What they do | Source status |
|---|---|---|
| Q1–Q2 | Check whether students tried to understand and imagine the negotiation from the other party's perspective. | Situational adaptations informed by the cognitive perspective-taking tradition, especially Davis (1983) and Galinsky et al. (2008). These are brief study-specific checks, not the full Interpersonal Reactivity Index. |
| Q3 | Asks whether students translated their understanding of the other party into questions, offers or responses. | Project-developed behavioral-enactment item. It deliberately distinguishes understanding a perspective from acting on it. |
| Q4 | Checks attention to one's own role and information. | Project-developed comparison item for the classroom preparation conditions. |
| Q5 | Measures satisfaction with one's own negotiation outcome. | Subjective Value Inventory item from Curhan, Elfenbein and Xu (2006). |
| Q6–Q7 | Compare satisfaction and realism with previous classroom negotiations where the issues and possible outcomes were fixed, such as New Recruit. | Project-developed comparative items. Analyze them individually rather than as a validated scale. |
| Q8–Q10 | Compare how creative students felt and their confidence in generating creative ideas and solving negotiation problems creatively. | Project-developed comparative creativity and creative-confidence items, not a validated creative-self-efficacy scale. |
| Q11–Q13 | Ask how students' strategies differed, which course principles were useful and what they would carry into future negotiations. | Instructor-developed reflection prompts, not a psychometric scale. |
| Q14–Q18 | Record whether and when the negotiation felt stuck, whether progress resumed, and how this compared with earlier fixed-issue negotiations. | Plain-language adaptation informed by Loewenstein and Brett (2007), plus project-developed comparison and no-stuck prompts. These remain after the comparison and reflection questions. |
| Q19–Q20 | Ask whether offer scores matched the interests and priorities in the role narrative, followed by an optional explanation. The rating has five agreement options and no Not applicable option. | Project-developed feedback items. The explanation is optional. |
| Q21–Q23 | Invite suggested changes to existing scores, new creative issues with proposed scores for each role, and any other feedback. | Optional, project-developed feedback prompts. Suggested values are opinions, not changes to the scoring table. |

Question numbers above follow the displayed order. Existing storage IDs are retained: the two added confidence questions use `q20` and `q21`, and the former general-feedback item `q17` has been removed. Questions about getting stuck still branch on `q12`.

## Prolific research survey

The 20 October payment notice appears before Q1 under the separate ID
`survey_bonus_information`. It is an optional acknowledgment, not a measure
or effort check. Q1–Q33, their wording, scale membership and branching stay
unchanged. The notice explains the current 50% / 25% / 15% / 10% survey-effort
bonus tiers; honest opinions and negative ratings do not reduce eligibility.

| Questions | What they do | Source status |
|---|---|---|
| Q1–Q16 | Measure feelings about the outcome, self, process and relationship. | Full 16-item Subjective Value Inventory from Curhan, Elfenbein and Xu (2006). Q3 and Q5 are reverse-scored. |
| Q17–Q18 | Check whether participants could imagine themselves in the assigned role and whether the negotiation felt realistic. | Q17 is a minimal role-specific adaptation of an established imaginability item; Q18 is a brief perceived-realism item. Analyze them separately unless a reliability check supports combining them. |
| Q19 | Checks whether participants took their assigned role seriously. | Project-developed role-engagement and data-quality item. |
| Q20–Q24 | Measure idea-generation engagement during the negotiation. | Five-item idea-generation component adapted to the negotiation from Zhang and Bartol's (2010) Creative Process Engagement measure. |
| Q25–Q27 | Measure confidence in solving problems creatively, developing another person's ideas and generating novel ideas. | Three-item Creative Self-Efficacy measure from Tierney and Farmer (2002), adapted to refer to this negotiation. |
| Q28 | Asks participants, without naming a particular strategy, how they decided what offer to make next. | Project-developed qualitative process question. Responses can be coded for exploitation of known terms, exploration of different terms, attention to feedback, concessions, interests and other emergent approaches. |
| Q29–Q32 | Record whether the negotiation felt stuck, whether this happened once or repeatedly, roughly when it happened, how or whether the parties began making progress again, and why some negotiations never felt stuck. | Plain-language adaptation informed by the post-negotiation protocol in Loewenstein and Brett (2007), plus a project-developed no-stuck prompt. The open responses can be coded for changing the issue set, trading across known issues, discussing interests, persistence, concession, smooth progress and unresolved disagreement. These questions are placed late to reduce priming. |
| Q33 | Collects optional feedback. | Project-developed. |
