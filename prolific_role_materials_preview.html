Your role

You are an ambitious job Candidate negotiating a sales role with a young company. You care about income, but money isn't all that matters in life. You want room to build a career, protect time with your family, and avoid getting stuck in a role where your work is visible but you are not developing professionally.

Your objective is to reach an agreement with the highest total score you can, while recognizing that the Recruiter has priorities of their own that may conflict with yours.

You prefer a higher salary and budget and an earlier start. Starting promptly would help restore your income and career momentum, while a long delay would be substantially more disruptive.

Salary and personal work-expense budget may be any non-negative amount. The figures below are scoring reference points, not limits on what you may propose.

  • Annual salary: $35,000 gives you −2 points,
    $50,000 gives you 0, and $65,000 gives you +2.
  • Starting date: January 1 gives you +1 point, March 1 gives you 0, May 1 gives you −1, July 1 gives you −2, and dates in September give you −3. Dates between these examples receive proportional points. A start after September means the deal cannot go ahead.
  • The personal budget: $5,000 gives you −2,
    $10,000 gives you 0, and $15,000 gives you +2.

Salary and budget amounts between these reference points receive intermediate scores. Amounts outside them continue to gain or lose points at the same rate.

For example, a $7,000 budget is worth about −1.2 points to you. You do not need to calculate this yourself.

These headline contract terms are easy to put numbers on. However, the interview also left you thinking about what the job would actually be like and what other terms you might ask for given what matters to you.

Background Information

On the way home, you think about the company's usual 30 vacation days per year and 3 remote-working days per week. Those arrangements seem reasonable. Each is worth 0 points to you.

Still, more flexibility would mean more time with your family and more room for interests and professional development outside work, while less flexibility would work against those interests.

  • For vacation, 10 days per year are worth −1 point to you and 50 days are worth +1.
  • For remote work, 1 day per week is worth −1 and all 5 working days are worth +1.

The two features are scored separately. Values between or beyond these examples continue at the same rate; vacation cannot exceed 365 days per year, and remote work cannot exceed the five working days per week.

You also remember the Recruiter's stories about how people grow in the company.

  • One new hire attended an external professional training program; another was paired with an experienced mentor.
  • Someone else built relationships with senior colleagues after volunteering at a recruitment fair.

Those examples appealed to you because they offered concrete ways to learn and build relationships rather than vague promises of “growth.”

Other stories were less appealing.

  • The Recruiter joked that sociable employees often end up organizing team events.
  • The company regularly shares strong client stories on social media. Employees close to those accounts may be asked to help shape the posts, and confident employees sometimes present at conferences.

… You enjoy people, but you do not want every spare hour turned into company visibility. A recruitment fair might expand your network; recurring publicity work might simply expand your workload.

That evening, your spouse asked whether the company would support the professional association that has helped you and what would happen if you eventually wanted to leave.

  • Sponsored membership could help keep your skills and outside relationships alive.
  • The question about leaving brought you back to the non-compete clause you had raised.
  • You want to show commitment without unnecessarily limiting your future.

The background explains why the terms in your private payoff table matter to you. The table lists the complete agenda and your own points, not the other party’s points.

Your score is private. Each point from your final agreement is worth US$1, up to a maximum negotiation bonus of US$15. This is separate from your base payment and any survey-effort bonus. Without an agreement, you receive no negotiation bonus.

Without a deal, you will continue your job search; you do not have another offer in hand. This fallback is worth 0 points.
